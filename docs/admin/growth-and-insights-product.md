# Growth & Insights | acquisition, player value and experimentation

**Status:** Proposed long-term operator product, 2026-11-14. Owner review required; no technical or code authority. **Owns:** the product questions, analytical experiences, cohorts, campaign performance and experiment interpretation for Product, Growth, Marketing and Leadership. **Does not own:** raw analytics infrastructure, email delivery, commercial ledgers, Trust profiling, private story inspection or release execution. References: [Commercial](commercial-operations-product.md), [LiveOps](live-operations-product.md), [Player 360](player-360-service-blueprint.md), [Trust & Privacy](trust-and-privacy-product.md) and [Command Center](command-center-product.md).

## 1. Purpose

The workspace answers: **Where do legitimate players come from, do they reach meaningful gameplay, do they return and enjoy it, why do they leave, and which changes improve their experience sustainably?**

This is not a page-view chart museum or a marketing license to read private fictional lives. The goal is trustworthy product learning with usable decisions and safeguards.

The game is unusual: a player can spend time in a story that advances through accepted offline work, have a quiet campaign with zero model calls for hours, or engage in multiple shared campaigns. Growth metrics must not confuse the number of generated passages with the number of delighted humans.

## 2. Five analytical lenses

| Lens | Operational question | Example evidence, where actually available |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acquisition | Who discovers the product and how? | Permitted traffic/campaign attribution, account creation |
| Activation | Who reaches a meaningful first authentic experience? | First valid creation and committed/played story interaction |
| Engagement | Are people voluntarily participating in their fictional lives? | Return visits, deliberate accepted choices, resumed activities and meaningful read events |
| Retention / reactivation | Do distinct cohorts return over time and why? | Cohort-specific person-level activity, churn/absence and verified return |
| Monetization / sustainability | Can valuable experiences be delivered profitably? | Conversion, renewal, actual grants/allowances, provider expense, service costs |

**Do not treat scripted public demo as a real paid-provider story, account signup as activation, a background timer as engagement, or internal troubleshooting as player activity.** A supporter invited to another person's story may be a real active player without owning or paying for a campaign.

## 4. A defensible metrics vocabulary

### Person, account, campaign and activity are separate units

A single person might use multiple accounts; a campaign can involve several players; an active campaign can progress in-fiction without a fresh human decision. The product must say which unit a metric counts.

Proposed **illustrative** definitions to be reviewed:
- **Registered accounts:** accounts with completed legitimate registration in an observation period; test/staff/fraud handling clearly disclosed.
- **First meaningful activation:** an eligible account enters a real persistent story and completes a defined meaningful player action or receives verified first valid play, depending on the product funnel chosen. The exact event is open, and never just a simulated public demo.
- **Human engagement:** a deliberate meaningful authorized interaction or return within a defined period, **not** model round count, worker heartbeat or mere offline time progression.
- **Campaign engagement:** distinct active campaigns based on explicit accepted/published gameplay, shown separately from people.
- **Retention:** among a fixed eligible cohort, fraction returning for a declared meaningful event during a later period.
- **Conversion:** eligible free/sponsored entity begins a valid paid relationship, with explicit unit and attribution window.
- **Churn:** depends on paid subscription renewal/cancellation rules or behavioral absence definition; these are different metrics.
- **Quality-adjusted value:** combine engagement with player feedback and mechanical reliability as separate dimensions rather than a speculative universal enjoyment score.

No final exact event names, 7/30-day policies or numerical success targets are approved here. Each chart must label what it actually measures and when it was last complete.

### Not all causal inferences are licensed

An uplift in retention after a release might reflect seasonality, acquisition mix, a concurrent promotion or a quality change. Show assignment, observation windows, comparability, sample sizes and known confounders. An experiment with insufficient sample is not "zero impact." Historical correlations are not proof of a cause.

## 5. Cohorts, segments and eligibility

A cohort is a **defined set for a stated analytical or operational purpose**, with versioned eligibility rules and a documented measurement time. A campaign audience adds actual deliverability/contact permissions, plan terms and restriction checks.

Important separation:
1. Analytics cohort: who belongs in a reporting population.
2. Experiment cohort: who was assigned and actually exposed to a variant.
3. Promotion audience: who may receive a particular commercial offering.
4. Contact audience: who may legitimately receive a message on a specified channel.

They may overlap but are **not interchangeable**. A player present in an analytics cohort is not automatically eligible for an email campaign, a price discount or a new risky game mechanic.

Because cohorts can expose identity by combining tiny segments, the operator experience should use appropriate minimum group sizes, protected dimensions, export controls and disclosure rules when necessary. Do not reveal private text or third-party participant identities through a clever filter.

## 6. Experiments and rollout handoff

A legitimate experiment record should contain:
- product hypothesis and expected user benefit;
- baseline and primary outcome measure;
- guardrails across mechanical correctness, privacy, model cost, latency, complaints and commercial promises;
- eligible population, consent and exclusions;
- actual assignment and **actual exposure**, not only intended percentage;
- sampling windows, known attribution gaps, crossover/spillover risks;
- decision to expand, pause, revise or discontinue, and a written rationale.

**Shared campaigns are not independent per-account experiments for every question.** Mechanics or world truth may need campaign-level consistency; the same world cannot validly follow conflicting rule systems for two participants merely because they were assigned separately.

Growth can propose and analyze. LiveOps controls eligible game-version activation; Commercial owns promotions and grant economics; Trust/Privacy constrains targeting and contact. Combining professional packages does not dissolve approval boundaries.

## 7. Marketing and customer communications

The operator can prepare audience definitions, offers and campaign objectives, see delivery and redemption evidence, and analyze results. Dedicated systems may perform email/notification distribution and analytics computation.

The Admin should distinguish drafted, approved, scheduled, triggered, sent, delivered, failed, opted-out and suppressed states **where supported by a real provider**. "Sent" is not necessarily "read" or "effective."

Customer Success outreach is a person-specific relationship workflow; Growth audience campaigns are scaled communications. Both must honor consent/contact policy and should not mine narrative content to infer psychological traits, medical state, relationships, sexuality or any other sensitive personal characteristics.

## 8. Worked journeys

### G-01: new players abandon during first story setup
Product examines a funnel from authentic entry → sign-in → prepared/custom creation → reviewed opening → first actual play. It segments only on legitimate attributes, distinguishes signed-in but ineligible (sponsored spend not available) from confused users, and links support themes without raw private content. An improvement is proposed and later measured.

**Negative:** a scripted demo click is not silently reported as first committed AI gameplay.

### G-02: a shorter Storyteller recipe saves money but worsens experience
AI Ops provides cost/latency by task; LiveOps knows actual release exposure; Product compares continuity failures, profile fidelity samples, human return and complaints. Finance assesses unit economics. A cheap route with more lost progress is not declared a win because average token count declined.

**Negative:** retained players alone are used to score story quality, excluding those who abandoned after the regression.

### G-03: a promotional trial for unactivated users
Growth proposes a cohort and offer; Commercial reviews eligibility and exposure; Trust/Privacy evaluates contact constraints; LiveOps controls any product-side assignment. Support can later explain to a specific player why an offer applied or did not apply.

**Negative:** canceling the campaign does not silently revoke an earned grant; a restricted account is not reactivated by marketing.

### G-04: a dormant player returns after weeks
Analysis distinguishes a person returning to read a continuing story, resuming accepted work, or actively selecting a new intention. A CS or campaign message is sent only under appropriate contact policy.

**Negative:** the world's offscreen progression alone is not counted as a daily returning user.

### G-05: mixed payer and participants
For a shared campaign the dashboard can report players, campaigns, payer relationships and beneficiaries separately. A plan tied to one payer cannot be counted as several new subscriptions merely because three friends joined.

**Negative:** cohort drilldown does not leak another participant's private profile or payment account.

## 9. Metric quality and governance

Each report/metric should expose:
- named owner and meaning; unit counted (person/account/campaign/story task/payment);
- definition, cohort and exclusion criteria;
- date range, timezone/reporting grain and freshness;
- completeness, delayed events, backfilled data and unknown portions;
- privacy threshold and whether drilldown/export is permitted;
- segmentation confounders and whether comparisons are observational or controlled.

An Insights Journal should preserve *what the team believed and why*, what decision was made, what changed and whether later evidence confirmed/refuted it. This is a business research workflow, not a claimed source of truth over raw gameplay or financial events.

## 10. Adverse cases

- Customer has paid but entitlement is pending; signup/paid conversion does not imply functional activation.
- A background routine runs for a week; player DAU remains based on actual human engagement, not worker ticks.
- One shared campaign has four members; campaign count is one while participant measures use their own denominator.
- A low-sample experiment sees apparent uplift; UI does not claim statistical certainty.
- An operator uses a tiny cohort to infer protected identity or story content; policy limits the drilldown.
- User opted out of outreach; campaign audience honors the suppression even if analytics include anonymized counts.
- A promotion is stopped but existing contractual/granted access persists according to approved terms.
- Attribution to a marketing source is missing; results show unattributed/unknown rather than assigning last-touch by fiat.
- An employee can analyze revenue trends but cannot issue refunds through the same dashboard.
- Staff/internal diagnostic activity is excluded or separately identified rather than polluting player conversion and usage.
