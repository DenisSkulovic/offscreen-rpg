# What we need to decide next

The product is concrete enough to begin technical design. These choices remain open; do not silently settle them by choosing a database schema or a scheduling library.

The [technical design](technical/architecture.md) now supplies implementation recommendations. In particular, [shared decisions](technical/client-and-identity.md), [timing](technical/execution.md) and [notifications](technical/notifications.md) contain concrete proposals for discussion, not assumed answers to the product choices below.

The first [scripted testing chamber](technical/delivery-and-validation.md#scripted-testing-chamber) uses explicit solo fixture rules to test persistence, choices and timing. That experiment does not require resolving every general product question below. Its policies remain visible and provisional rather than silently becoming defaults for all stories.

## Settle before implementing the affected behavior

1. **Shared decisions and membership:** how are individual intentions collected and conflicting actions resolved? Who can start, pause and resume the group story? Begin with one shared situation and an invited pair; choose the supported initial group limit. Future large worlds need an explicit no-global-pause/absence policy and separate scene coordination; the hundred-player ambition is not the initial limit. The technical proposal starts with joining during setup; decide what happens to a character/group when a participant leaves or the owner deletes their account.
2. **Agency between decisions:** the owner selected pre-generated contextual choices, with no free-text gameplay actions, for the POC on 2026-09-18. Premise/direction entry during setup remains text. When may someone interrupt a quiet interval? Define commitment and frequency limits without reducing interaction to endless regeneration.
3. **Pace and deadlines:** solo rate presets, instant boundaries and explicit active-activity rescheduling are designed in [settings execution](technical/story-settings.md). Shared-clock authority, combat timing and general interruption remain open; narrative prepared waits retain their separate policy.
4. **Autonomy and risk:** which choices can a character make without its player, and which permanent consequences may the storyteller introduce during absence? These are separate permissions. What should happen when the available fallback exceeds permission or a player is absent for a long time?
5. **Rules and possessions:** D&D is required, not optional. The [POC subset](game-rules.md) selects ability/skill checks and counted rewards. The selected baseline is SRD 5.2.1, revised fifth edition; the implemented subset must remain explicit. Full combat, advancement, loss/death rules and richer inventory remain later feature decisions.
6. **Spending and recovery:** what are the initial generation and ongoing budgets, and who funds a shared story? The technical proposal shows a specific blocker when progression cannot continue. Decide whether restored allowance/provider availability should automatically resume that held story or wait for the player; it must never clear a manual pause. Development uses an explicit developer-funded cap without deciding commercial tiers. For the hosted portfolio trial, choose visitor eligibility, allowance/expiry and an aggregate site cap; roughly $2 per visitor is an example, not a committed entitlement.
7. **Notifications:** which first channel reaches someone away from the browser, and does it link back or support decisions directly? A workplace messaging integration is a candidate, not a selected dependency.
8. **Endings:** continuing life without a compulsory ending is a target. How should creation distinguish it from a finite adventure, and how can the player deliberately conclude it? A chapter or adventure ending must not implicitly terminate a continuing life. The short chamber still ends to test terminal behavior.
9. **Space, scenes and movement:** what is the smallest authoritative representation of places, connections, current scene staging and interrupted journeys, including two characters travelling independently? Start by validating the bounded scene-frame proposal before selecting a map representation. Choose how duration/progress are established and how exceptional movement works before implementing travel. A uniform 3D cube grid is a candidate map format; physical scale, sparse space worlds, abstract connections and optional regional/local maps still need validation. Cinematic shot/composition data is derived presentation, not world truth. The interactive map itself is deferred and must not force a universal geometry onto all stories. See [places, scenes and cinematic presentation](continuity-and-consequences.md#places-scenes-and-cinematic-presentation), [spatial continuity](continuity-and-consequences.md#space-movement-and-maps) and the [data boundary](technical/data.md).
10. **Process progress and capacity:** ticks schedule advancement but do not measure work. Review the proposed [activity-process architecture](features/2026-09-18--16-48--activity-processes-and-progress/FEATURE.md): which initial contribution, waiting and traversal fixtures should prove it, and what is the smallest capacity-allocation model needed without assuming one human body or unlimited parallel action?

## Refine through the first experience

Exact storyteller copy, initial example premises, image frequency and the placement of sign-in can be tested without postponing the whole technical design. The owner confirmed that storytellers are data over generic runtime behavior; the particular two sample styles are not an architectural decision. The implemented profile/runtime, continuity and local solo POC boundaries are described in the permanent storyteller and technical documents. Reusable custom storytellers and separate simultaneous party adventures do not need to be designed now.
