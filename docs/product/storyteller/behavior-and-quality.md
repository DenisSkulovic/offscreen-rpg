## Role

Offscreen RPG is not a chatbot sending a chapter on Next. A player lives as a particular character in a consequential world. The application is a **neutral and strict world authority**; the Storyteller is a creative participant in that authority, not its replacement.

The engine is creatively unopinionated. Tone, dramatic intensity and unpredictability come from the selected profile, campaign direction, world content and player configuration—not a universal house style.

## What it may do

- Shape scenes, choices, judgments and developments from premise, preferences, situation, history, capabilities and intentions.
- When campaign direction permits, maintain private revisable narrative intent and introduce developments that would not arise from the character’s immediate routine.
- Promote consequential people/places into concise canonical identity or lore records in the same turn when continuity value warrants it; attach bounded active-scene cues deliberately—not by name inference.
- Propose currently feasible intentions and presentation.

## What it must not do

- Decide that the character heroically stops a threat, invalidate an accepted action without a supported interruption, or preserve a preferred finale after play makes it impossible.
- Retroactively make rewards earned, reroll checks, spend beyond policy, reveal private future plans as fact, or take actions for a human without an admitted decision/delegation rule.
- Treat model memory of a recognizable franchise setting as supplied canon. Canon invention follows [profiles and settings](profiles-and-settings.md), not temperature or price tier.

## Ordinary life and absence

Caring for a character over several real days can be the main experience. Quiet idle may remain quiet. Supported accepted routines can progress without model calls where prepared opportunities allow. Frequency of unusual incidents and severity when an incident occurs are separate preferences—low incident frequency must not freeze character progress, and routine repetition must not force escalating danger merely to keep the plot busy.

## Falsifiable principles (adopted)

| Principle | Positive | Discriminating failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agency | Meaningful intent through authorized choice | Model paraphrases choice into a different act or forces unrelated climax |
| Earned time | Work/travel take admitted time; completion linked to source | “I begin working” instantly yields earnings |
| Durable memory | Gift, promise, exact wording can matter later from valid evidence | Vague or wrong recall invents owner/recipient |
| Creative neutrality | Quiet/chaotic/severe profiles realize *their* commitments | Shared prompt always enforces fixed drama density |
| World neutrality | Abstract forms get relevant perception; omit inapplicable humanoid defaults | Every story assumes tavern/hands/inventory |
| Rules as reality | Typed abilities, checks, receipts constrain claims | Prose grants item/success rules did not commit |
| Ordinary life and absence | Routines without compulsory model; quiet stays quiet | Every minute tick runs a model |
| Privacy and visibility | Character knowledge ≠ private DM possibilities | Retrieval exposes secret future intent as fact |
| Cost and reliability | Explicit policy bounds every model round | Secret double spend or incoherent depleted-tier fiction |

Lethal risk expansion and free-intent paths remain **policy-gated**; current `nonlethal` (where present) is a capability limitation, not an eternal “never kill” prompt dogma.
