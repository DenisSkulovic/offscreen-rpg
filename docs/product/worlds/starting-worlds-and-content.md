# Starting a story, not generating a simulator

D064 asks for a lighter initial preparation contract. This revision recommends a playable opening, compact character context and enough lore to continue coherently. It does not select a schema, model pipeline or universal-mechanics generator.

## Three starting examples

An almost undefined opening, a custom premise and an established setting can all begin with the same story-progression flow. Establish only what is needed to orient the character and offer meaningful intentions. Preserve deliberate user-specified material. A broad setting need not have a map, generated residents or a catalogue of executable activities before the first scene.

## What initial material can mean

Keep desired premise, established fact, source reference, character belief, hidden fact and unspecified detail distinct. A previously defined hidden room is not the same as room content not yet invented. Model recollection of a fictional setting is not verified canon.

## Proposed behavior

- WORLD-START-001: Missing distant detail does not block a coherent opening.
- WORLD-START-002: Preserve deliberately established facts; later invention must respect them.
- WORLD-START-003: Do not present invented or recalled lore as verified source material.
- WORLD-START-004: Distinguish setting conventions, adopted character/check rules and storyteller judgment; explain material conflicts.
- WORLD-START-005: Generated consequential references must resolve to the intended entities or explicitly unspecified detail. Do not generate a whole faction just to validate one name.
- WORLD-START-006: Bound generation, repair and expense. Unfinished output is not committed play. Cancellation and partial retry preserve accepted facts.
- WORLD-START-007: Prepare a short playable opening and only useful pending continuation; do not pre-generate an entire campaign or branch tree.

## Options and forks

WORLD-F01 owns starting detail: sparse scene, local setting or richer optional lore. WORLD-F02/F03 remain in [authorship and fidelity](invention-discovery-and-canon.md#authorship-and-fidelity-forks). WORLD-F04 concerns conflicting starting facts: clarify material contradictions, infer small compatible details. Exact defaults remain open.

## Mechanical variety and simple setup

### A shared game contract with specific world rules

Recommend a short campaign premise: character/background, relevant capabilities, tone, autonomy, adjudication/check policy and time policy. The storyteller can interpret scenes across worlds without implementing each possible activity or physical domain. Preserve established constraints, such as which powers the character has; avoid promising precise physical simulation or complete source compliance.

### Draft preparation journey

1. Interpret the desired character and premise, inferring compatible low-risk details.
2. Establish relevant starting facts, capabilities, possessions if useful, and campaign limits.
3. Generate the current scene and a few meaningful intentions with time estimates; a broad routine is an ongoing intention, not a generated scheduler program.
4. Check references, consistency and executable generic transitions; explain material unsupported expectations.
5. Begin the story. Expand relevant lore and cast when needed; reuse accepted context.

The validation target is coherent next play under the minimal contract, not proof that every lore noun has a bespoke simulator. Do not execute arbitrary generated code or build an activity catalogue to make every premise look supported.

### Composition and transitions need rules

Keep only constraints that materially affect a current choice: identity, relevant containment, time, significant possessions and lasting conditions. A change of body/scale can be narratively adjudicated where the campaign allows it. Accurate mass, atmosphere, spacecraft and molecular physics are not implied. Recurring consequential powers may acquire explicit limits as needed; no exhaustive combination matrix is required.

WORLD-F05 is reopened by D064: prefer broad bounded narrative adjudication plus a small stable fact/check contract as a proposal. Compare consistency and cost against a stricter supported-mechanics approach before claiming this balance is selected. Additional lore richness is optional work with its own budget; large setup output is not a success metric.
