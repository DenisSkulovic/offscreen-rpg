# World events and causality

This topic owns storyteller pacing and incident authorship. “Supported consequence” can mean a coherent generic state change adjudicated by the model; it does not require a dedicated activity or combat subsystem.

Owner: Denis. Release disposition: unassigned.
Scope: developments beyond the player's immediate action and their effects.
Sources: [principles](../foundations/product-principles.md), [world facts](invention-discovery-and-canon.md), [time](../experience/time-presence-and-autonomy.md), [knowledge](../characters-and-autonomy/knowledge-beliefs-and-secrets.md).

Confirmed direction: the player sometimes drives events and sometimes becomes caught in them. Ordinary livelihoods and wider developments should coexist. ## What an event contributes

An event changes the character's circumstances or the world around them. It may follow a player's action, another actor's choice, an established process, or chance allowed by the campaign.

A player plan does not reserve future world conditions. External developments may prevent its execution or create a chain of new circumstances; the character’s responses can then change the course again. [Goals and orders](../characters-and-autonomy/goals-orders-and-permissions.md#plans-meet-a-changing-world) owns the effect on plan pursuit.

A larger development can matter through ordinary life: access to work, travel conditions, available goods, relationships or personal obligations. It need not immediately become a quest or combat encounter.

Not every event requires a player decision, narration or a notification. Which events are surfaced and when belongs in the planned notification and presentation topics.

## Illustrative situations

**A route becomes unavailable.** A traveler may discover the problem on arrival or hear about it earlier. The response depends on location, available information and choices. This example does not select a weather system or mandatory roadblock events.

**A dispute affects work.** Other characters' decisions change a job opportunity. The player might engage, seek other work or remain unaware until affected. It need not be rewritten as a heroic mission.

**A distant conflict changes local life.** Consequences may arrive through people, trade or institutions before direct danger arrives. This illustrates several possible scales, not a commitment to simulate every army or economy.

**An unexpected gain.** Good fortune is possible too. Its resolution still follows campaign rules and circumstances; randomness is not permission for the narrator to grant arbitrary requested rewards.

## A character inside an unfolding history

Denis’s world-generation analogy clarifies the desired relationship: the character inhabits an unfolding history rather than a backdrop that changes only when they act. Developments can occur beyond the character, pass through their life, and be changed by their actions in return. Personal history and world history are connected.

This is an experience direction, not a requirement to pre-generate centuries, wait through lengthy setup, or simulate every inhabitant at equal detail. It must also coexist with the accepted ability to start with almost undefined content. The campaign clock still governs progression; whole-world pause freezes historical developments too.

The player need not witness or learn every remote event. What happened, what reaches their circumstances, and what they believe happened may differ. The extent and timing of historical definition remain open; do not select a full civilization simulator merely from the analogy.

## Consequence chains and uncertainty

One event may affect several people or activities. The product must eventually decide how much of that chain matters in play; it need not describe every causal step.

The character may receive an incomplete or false explanation. That is compatible with a coherent underlying event; the player's ability to inspect hidden causes is owned by KNOW-F05. Do not require omniscient explanations as the price of believable consequences.

## Proposed Dungeon Master role

D043–D046 establish an LLM-based storyteller that originates incidents and develops stories beyond ordinary routines. It can invent compatible circumstances and participants when introducing a supported incident; it need not wait for existing NPCs or factions to independently cause it. Inspect relevant facts to preserve continuity, then originate a development or continuation and supply expressive framing. It can have access to selected world secrets for planning without granting that knowledge to NPCs or the player. Its purpose is coherent opportunities and consequences; quiet periods are valid outcomes.

D059–D060 make this the primary world-building philosophy rather than an optimization around a hidden exhaustive simulation. Like a human dungeon master, the storyteller keeps the cast, open threads, current situation and useful larger plans in view, then invents compatible detail at the edge of play. It does not need 100,000 simulated NPCs so that one interesting encounter can statistically fall out. This is deliberate authorship, not proof that all prior causes were computed.

EVENT-006: Distinguish a proposal, an admitted event, its resolved consequences and its presentation. The director may choose among permitted developments or propose new compatible content under the authorship contract. Check the proposed changes against established facts, tracked resources, adopted roll results and campaign policy before they become authoritative. The storyteller may judge contextual feasibility; the application checks what it can actually enforce and records effects once. Structural checks alone cannot prove narrative coherence. Whether an effect is expressed through detailed or coarse rules depends on supported mechanics, not on how dramatic its description is.

EVENT-007: A storyline is an evolving set of circumstances and unresolved possibilities, not a predetermined ending. Player decisions, NPC actions and chance can derail it. Track only relevant unresolved threads; close or abandon obsolete ones instead of keeping every plot alive or calling a model on every tick. Do not undo a valid escape because a planned kidnapping chapter needs it.

EVENT-008: Director proposals have context and timing constraints. Revalidate if the player acts, state changes, speed changes or the world pauses before admission. Late model output cannot overwrite resolved history or advance a paused world. Routine simulation may continue without optional directing; lack of a model response is not a fictional event. The precise conflict/retry mechanism is technical work.

### Overnight surprise, illustrated

A possible raid illustrates the boundary: the storyteller may introduce attackers and the incident together, with a setting-compatible arrival and supported conflict resolution. No prior bandit organization, leader decision, recruitment economy or simulated journey is required. Relevant entry/location constraints and previously established facts still apply; do not describe an impossible entrance or contradict a known eliminated group. Background motivation can be authored economically, without simulating its history. The active policy might pause before danger, permit an authorized defensive/default response, or permit unattended resolution with serious losses. Capture can occur only if capture, custody/location change and subsequent player options actually exist in the chosen mechanics. Do not declare captivity from narration while inventory, location and available actions still describe an ordinary day at home.

When the player returns, report committed outcomes and what the character can know, with a usable next step. No outcome is guaranteed: the attack may fail, be avoided or never be selected. This is a design stress example, not a required raid/captivity feature. Muting phone notifications does not grant broader autonomy; sleeping in real life does not itself select either protection or danger.

### Proposed first-release depth

Recommend a small supported incident set with LLM-based selection/authorship, plus procedural scheduling, validation and a low-cost fallback. The LLM should contribute actual incident content or selection rather than merely rename a fixed notification. Choose a development whose consequences the initial action set can actually resolve; do not require combat or kidnapping to prove the director works. Demonstrate interruption of a routine, a meaningful choice/default and persistent consequences. D045 settles permission to originate incidents. EVENT-F03 retains detailed steering limits; event cadence, preset defaults and unattended risk settings still require a product choice. A multi-agent society and unconstrained world-authoring god are not first-release requirements.

## Storyteller presets and sparse world simulation

The world has two complementary sources of change: ordinary rule-driven activity for currently relevant state and storyteller-introduced incidents. Existing causes can also supply story opportunities, but exhaustive causal simulation is unnecessary and is not the product's default architecture. The storyteller can introduce a visitor, opportunity, shortage or threat within the supported world profile. Creating permitted external participants/resources is explicit world authorship, not a transfer out of an imaginary pre-simulated inventory. Once admitted, consequential entities, possessions, relationships and outcomes persist normally. Do not repeatedly generate duplicate identities or erase incident participants after they become relevant.

Proposed preset dimensions are event cadence/recovery space, predictability, tone, preferred incident themes and preference for continuing unresolved threads versus introducing new ones. Illustrative styles: a quiet chronicler, an arc-building dramatist and an unpredictable instigator. These are proposals, not selected names or a required three-preset launch catalogue. Presets must change actual selection/pacing behavior, not only narration voice.

Keep storyteller style distinguishable from severity, unattended permissions, notification frequency and inference budget. An unpredictable style does not automatically permit unattended death or unlimited calls; a peaceful style can still introduce interesting relationships and opportunities. A shared scheduler can enforce cooldowns, incident concurrency and cost limits while an LLM selects/authors within those limits. Quiet periods remain legitimate.

Recommended initial switching policy: apply the new style to future selections, preserve admitted events/history and existing spending limits, and carry forward recent-event pressure so switching cannot repeatedly reset a grace period or trigger event bursts. Exact policy remains proposed. Higher spend may improve variety or continuity; it must not secretly mean more danger.

Reference checked 2026-09-16: the official [RimWorld description](https://rimworldgame.com/) describes a storyteller that analyzes the player's situation and chooses events, with carefully paced Cassandra, slower Phoebe and unpredictable Randy. This supports the incident-director analogy; it is not evidence that every RimWorld event has the same origin or proof of its detailed implementation. Our LLM-based creative role is our own design.

## Recurring cast and object continuity

D047–D048 let the storyteller draw on a persistent cast without running each member continuously. Prefer relevant established connections when useful; do not force every old acquaintance into a dramatic return. Before reusing someone, distinguish their identity and binding facts from uncertain whereabouts and unspecified activity. Retrieve consequential connections beyond the recent conversation window; budget limits can reduce elaboration, not authorize contradictions.

The golden-spear example: the sale establishes a transfer to Bob. Twelve fictional days later, a storyteller may introduce Bob's involvement in a new incident without simulating his intervening meals, travel steps or recruitment meetings. Reusing that same spear requires consistency with its established custody and any intervening loss, transfer or destruction. If a supported change of allegiance or compatible off-screen development is needed, establish it without contradicting prior facts. The intended dramatic connection does not guarantee a successful attack or injury; resolve the incident under supported mechanics and policy. Neither a full bandit society nor this particular encounter is required.

## Everyday attachment, tone and incidents that unfold

D051–D052 emphasize attachment built through ordinary routines, recurring relationships and small encounters, followed by developments that can be funny, strange, tragic or frightening. Preserve enough continuity for the change to matter. Do not force every relationship into a future victim or turn every quiet period into a countdown to catastrophe. Severe outcomes and unpreventable surprises may be authored where the supported campaign contract permits them; neither universal protection nor constant cruelty is the default implied by these examples.

EVENT-009: An incident may span several stages, with actions, elapsed fictional time, persistent consequences and new decisions between them. A selected defensive action can fail later under its actual conditions. The storyteller adapts to resolved state rather than forcing a predetermined ending. Some stages may resolve through rules without another model call; model-authored continuations must remain within budget and current facts.

Distinguish emotional tone, consequence severity, descriptive intensity and intervention policy. A horrific campaign can use restrained descriptions; absurd presentation need not mean harmless outcomes. These are proposed configuration dimensions, not a requirement for four new settings screens. Known relationships and severe changes need actual supported state; a spouse or injury named in prose does not establish an implemented family/body system. Initial incidents can demonstrate lasting consequences through supported resources, access or relationships, without requiring the illustrative violence mechanics.

Storyteller authority to originate an incident does not require every harm to have a simulated causal prehistory or prior player warning. It does require a permitted supported effect and consistent committed state. Notifications report that state and offer remaining agency; a countdown is not a promise that the entire tragedy can be undone.

## Variable intensity with a life between incidents

D057 describes a changing rhythm: uneventful life, small developments, sustained action, aftermath and travel. Storyteller presets can shape that rhythm without requiring a fixed dramatic cycle or escalating every session. An ongoing incident may supply multiple developments; it need not generate a fresh unrelated crisis for every report. Allow resolved incidents to have an aftermath and quiet space. Player choices and autonomous outcomes can redirect or end an episode; narrative plans do not guarantee survival or death. Ordinary unattended continuation is central, with explicit pause available.
