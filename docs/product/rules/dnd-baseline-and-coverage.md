# Character checks and the D&D boundary

Earlier direction adopted D&D as a foundation, while D064 now asks how much machinery is actually necessary and suggests abilities/skills/checks as a useful starting point. This is a reopening of scope, not an approved replacement system or a claim that the user selected an edition.

## Source decision

RULES-F01: Choose the smallest explicit source subset useful to character identity and uncertain actions. Recommend considering attributes, skills/proficiencies and a small check contract before classes, spells, combat rounds or movement tables. Verify source/version and licensing before copying rules or claiming compliance. No edition or numeric formula is selected here.

## Coverage contract

For any check actually adopted, explain eligibility, applicable character traits, difficulty authority, roll semantics and how results constrain narration. Record the result once; retries must not reroll an unchanged situation. Difficulty and stakes should be set before resolving the roll. A routine obvious action need not have a check merely because it has a verb.

Storyteller judgment can resolve situations outside a coded subsystem. Distinguish adopted D&D checks from our custom story pacing, luck/event selection and duration estimates; do not label every random encounter or personality choice as a D&D rule. Character competence, success at an action and the arrival of unrelated trouble are different questions.

## Extension boundary

Recommend bounded narrative adjudication: the model judges feasibility, duration and consequences in light of abilities, established facts and campaign permissions; the application preserves generic state changes, time and any adopted roll result. This avoids requiring dedicated travel, employment, trade, survival or combat implementations for every narrated situation.

Adjudication is not unlimited player-authored success. A proposed capability must be compatible with the character and situation; a claimed effect needs an understandable consequence. A recurring power whose limits materially affect choices may need a small persistent rule, without requiring an entire magic system. Precise tactical behavior should only be promised where it is actually implemented.

RULES-F02: Decide where stable character checks improve the lightweight experience versus free storyteller judgment. Recommend a small consistent check vocabulary, with optional inspectable roll explanations rather than mandatory dice display. Rich tactical combat, classes, spell lists, equipment statistics, movement and economic source coverage are optional later work, not initial prerequisites.
