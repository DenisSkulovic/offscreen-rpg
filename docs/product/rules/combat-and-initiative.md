# Combat and initiative

This document owns the encounter contract. Exact initiative, action economy, round durations, checks, damage and recovery must come from the chosen source or explicit adaptations; none is invented here. SCN-004 supplies a small encounter fixture.

## Proposed encounter contract

Establish participants, actual positions/containment, relevant knowledge, capabilities, resources and applicable effects. Determine action order under the adopted rules. When an actor may act, determine legal attempts using reach/range, target availability, environment, action/resource limits and active conditions. Narrated intent alone does not establish an extra action or a reachable target.

The player or autonomy policy selects the attempt. Resolve its checks, resource use and effects under the adopted rules. Apply consequences once, then present the actor-permitted outcome. A model may interpret intent, select a bounded legal action or describe a result; it cannot rewrite order, replenish resources or grant extra attacks.

Combat is later-candidate scope. Its direct-play waiting policy must be selected with its source/action model; the superseded general MVP global-pause recommendation does not decide combat timing. Only resolved fictional action/round duration advances concurrent campaign processes; model latency is not extra enemy time. Late input cannot revise a completed action. An unavailable presentation may fall back to facts; an unresolved essential choice follows the configured pause/autonomy policy.

Resolve retreat, incapacitation, defeat and encounter termination explicitly. Continued activity after the encounter uses actual location, health, resources and conditions, not a narrative reset. Recovery remains governed by supported source rules and declared consequence settings.

## Coverage and open choice

COMBAT-F01: Select the initial participant capabilities, positional model and supported spells/effects together with RULES-F01. Include normal attacks, a blocked/out-of-range attempt, resource exhaustion and withdrawal/defeat in the coverage examples. A modern weapon, spacecraft or microscopic hazard requires supported behavior and interactions; a different label alone does not supply them.

A hazard need not have intentions or take humanoid turns. Profile-specific adaptations must make its interaction with the encounter contract explicit. Exact numerical balance and full spell/creature coverage remain unselected.

Release clarification (D040–D041): the initial livelihood slice adopts only mechanics needed for its supported actions. Full combat coverage is a later candidate; this document must not be interpreted as a combat prerequisite for first delivery. DOC-RELEASE-SCOPE owns release placement.
