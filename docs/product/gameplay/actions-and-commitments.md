# Actions and commitments | intention to continuation

**Owner (DOC01 P2):** how player intentions become accepted work and what may continue without fresh inference. Reconciles progression sections of former `foundations/gameplay.md`.  
**Companions:** [rules](rules.md), [time and autonomy](time-and-autonomy.md), [concepts](../foundations/concepts.md).  
**Technical:** [solo gameplay](../../technical/engine/authority-and-transitions.md), [rules and activities](../../technical/engine/mechanics-and-capabilities.md#prepared-local-opportunities).

## One generic progression flow

Walking, noticing a tower, making an agreement, spending an afternoon and meeting a stranger can all become moments in the same chronology. The product does not require a dedicated software system for each verb. No chapters, literary boundaries or fixed turn durations are implied.

The Storyteller prepares a **turn**; the player receives narration and, when interaction is needed, a **decision point**. Multiple turns can share a scene. Selected actions consume declared fictional time; reading and model processing do not.

## From intention to continuation

The Storyteller receives the current situation, relevant memories, character capabilities, campaign settings and a bounded selection of canonical material—not an entire setting dump. It offers contextual intentions and can prepare reusable local activities. Supported rules adjudicate a selected attempt and commit checks and effects; narration follows only when accepted policy calls for it. Timed activities resolve at meaningful game-time boundaries, not through one unconstrained prose prediction.

Player input expresses an **intention**; it does not directly supply resulting world changes. Neither repeated clicks nor reloading may produce free rerolls, duplicate actions or endless regeneration of suggestions.

**Negative:** a restart or repeated click must not apply the same outcome twice. A planned arrival is not a completed journey; changing course may invalidate a prepared encounter on the abandoned route.

## Prepared local life while the Storyteller is dormant

On establishing a relevant situation, the Storyteller can prepare a bounded set of opportunities (fish, tavern, rumors, rest). The game validates supported rules, targets, outcomes, eligibility and wake conditions. The player may discover, select, repeat or switch among still-valid prepared activities **without fresh inference**.

The Storyteller authors available intentions for each situation; the engine does **not** invent options from inventory, capabilities or physical feasibility. Owning an apple does not add “Eat apple.” The engine may filter or reject an authored choice whose conditions fail, but cannot substitute its own choice or expand the menu.

Each new interactive scene explicitly establishes its available choices. Earlier local activities do not automatically reappear because an encounter resolved. Within a still-authorized quiet situation, the player can repeatedly use exactly the prepared opportunities without another model call.

Known activities can end quietly, produce bounded mechanical results, continue accepted work, or request narration under configured policies. Wakes are bounded and deduplicated—not model polling. **No event is also a valid result.**

Routine chitchat does not promise fresh bespoke dialogue; repeated rumor gathering cannot manufacture unlimited novel lore for free.

> Implementation honesty: this direction is richer than today’s scene-local offer system where that system has not yet landed the full prepared-opportunity contract. See technical opportunity contract.

## Storyteller-directed pace

The game must support both rapid narration→choice sequences and hours-long offered activities while the player is elsewhere. They are not a compulsory alternating cycle. Scene resolution does not automatically return to activities; activity completion does not automatically request a scene. Brief dialogue, observation and combat actions still have explicit time costs; menu navigation consumes no fictional time.

## What the game remembers (commitment view)

- **History:** what actually happened.
- **Current situation:** whereabouts, conditions, relationships, capabilities, possessions.
- **Pending continuation:** what might happen next and under what circumstances.

An authored start assembles only content actually present: an abstract consciousness does not acquire a humanoid body, inventory, map or job merely because another start uses them. Starting a campaign makes an independent pinned copy of campaign-owned material.

## Judgment, chance and possessions

Whether an action succeeds and whether an interesting encounter occurs are **different questions**. Not every mundane action needs a roll. When a roll determines something, its result constrains the story rather than being ignored or rerolled.

A compact possession list may suffice; keep identity for significant objects and quantity when choices depend on count. Contextual bargains (coins, barter, favor) need consistent recording—not a universal price system. Capacity rules and merchant economies wait until play needs them.

## Keeping inference affordable

Prepare a short stretch after an intention is selected rather than generating every possible future. Waiting, publishing a valid scheduled update and applying an agreed change do not require another model call. If generation is unavailable or budget exhausted, use only still-valid prepared content or a supported fallback—**do not invent progress or silently exceed the budget**.

## Open / deferred product policy

Agency between offered decisions (free-intent paths, changing course mid-activity, GM07-style delegated decisions) remains explicitly unsettled where not accepted elsewhere. Do not treat program drafts as silent acceptance.
