They are examples, not technical law and not recorded live sessions. The accumulated-life atlas is [Long-life benchmark playthroughs](long-life-benchmark-playthroughs.md).

**Route.** This page holds four things, in this order of authority for the first playable milestone:

1. [Storyteller refoundation acceptance narratives](#storyteller-refoundation-acceptance-narratives): five authored target narratives (Prepared Play, `BAH`, conscious galaxy, two-hundred-scene callback, fork at scene 137).
2. The first solo acceptance oracle, [Harbor and return](#first-solo-acceptance-walkthrough--harbor-and-return).
3. The later wizard, companion and goblin stress vignette, sections [1](#1-arrive-and-create)–[6](#6-return-and-conclude), kept for shared decisions, a changed body and a missed window.
4. The [short obligation list](#what-the-first-playable-build-should-demonstrate) and a pointer to the [long-life benchmark playthroughs](#long-life-benchmark-playthroughs).

**Status of every scenario here.** Each is an authored target: an acceptance oracle read as product intent. None is a logged run, a recorded live session, a passing test, an implementation plan or technical law. Characters, wording, durations and outcomes are illustrative. They do not require goblin mechanics, apple mechanics or separate software concepts called nodes and events. Unsettled behavior is identified where it affects the experience.

**Shared play and delegation are not part of the first solo target.** The invited friend and a companion acting at the same time are beyond the solo [first playable target](../foundations/vision.md#first-playable-target). They illustrate the distant small-party aspiration; they are not required before the first solo story and they are not implemented. A missed-window autonomous choice is an unsettled delegation protocol. [What it must not do](../storyteller/behavior-and-quality.md#what-it-must-not-do) forbids taking a human's action without an admitted delegation rule. [First playable scope](time-and-autonomy.md#first-playable-scope) keeps an unanswered decision open. The exact live acceptance for that group is Proposed/Blocked. A goblin deadline in the scenes below is illustration, not selected delegated-decision policy.

The [gameplay execution atlas](../../../research/authored-scenarios/README.md) is the technical companion: concrete scenes/options, exact worked dice and timing, activity interruptions/chains, Storyteller context/tools and illustrative token ledgers for the near-term scenes below and the [long-life benchmark playthroughs](long-life-benchmark-playthroughs.md), plus SpongeBob and the beacon fixtures. Read these high-level examples for the feeling, then the corresponding trace to examine authority and implementation gaps. The [proposed connected POC](../../../research/authored-scenarios/poc.md) selects a small proof without making every example a release requirement.

These playthroughs are a living product reference, not disposable scaffolding for the current implementation. Preserve and maintain them as behavior evolves. Add or extend a benchmark when a new story, world, kind of character, chronology or social arrangement puts a genuinely different assumption under pressure. A benchmark may remain deliberately ahead of the runtime; its gap should stay visible and link to the owning contract rather than being erased or rewritten as though the limitation were intended.

**How to read a scene.** `solo V0` is texture for the first tested solo milestone. It is not an executed test. `beyond V0` is a feature combination that milestone does not require. A vivid sentence does not implement a map, a co-op resolver, or a biological ruleset. In sections 1–6 the **Stage / Missing / Failure / Provider shape** line is a reading aid: **Provider shape** describes the kind of model work the story implies and is illustrative, not an imposed model-call count or a selected orchestration.

## Storyteller refoundation acceptance narratives

These are **authored target scenarios**, not claims of current implementation or passed tests. They complement the harbor fixture by exercising assumptions the old wizard/character flow could not.

### Prepared Play without setup ceremony

A player selects a fully authored experience and presses **Play**. Its Library already contains orientation, creative guidance and starting material. No mandatory setup model call exists merely to manufacture fields the author already supplied. The opening mission reads the pinned sources it needs and publishes the first scene directly. Optional Customize remains optional.

### One-word original world

The player enters `BAH` as the complete Story Prompt. The system preserves that text verbatim as intent. The Storyteller may invent a coherent original fiction rather than rejecting it for lack of title, class, character sheet or lore package. The admitted opening establishes only the facts it actually needs.

### Conscious galaxy / observer perspective

The player asks to experience a conscious galaxy observing civilizations inside itself. The story does not fabricate hands, inventory, profession, tavern choices or six D&D abilities. Meaningful choices fit the perspective: attend to a signal, reshape a stellar region if the world's capabilities allow it, communicate, wait, observe, or pursue another fictionally valid intention.

### Two-hundred-scene callback

Much later, the Storyteller needs the exact wording and circumstances of an old promise. The mission does not receive 200 scenes in its initial prompt. It discovers likely Library sources, reads the exact historical evidence, optionally consults a continuity-writing skill, stages/revises a note or draft, and writes the continuation with source-grounded recall. A separate bounded assistant may investigate another thread without inheriting the parent's full context.

### Fork during an unfinished journey

At scene 137 a caravan has completed part of a long journey, carries an accepted obligation, and still has unresolved work. The player forks scene 137. Both branches inherit exactly the facts, progress, clock and source versions that existed there. The child creates fresh operational ownership for remaining work; it does not copy parent provider attempts, leases, billing identity or knowledge from scenes 138+. Parent and child may then diverge independently.

A system that can show old prose but cannot faithfully resume the admitted active fiction at that point has historical viewing, not the target fork contract.

## First solo acceptance walkthrough | harbor and return

**Stage:** authored solo V0 target. **Missing:** a logged run of this story, a provider-connected quality judgment, and a proved restart through the published snapshot. **Failure:** reading the harbor prose as acceptance, or treating a synthetic receipt as a human play. **Provider shape:** none in this page. The arithmetic lives in [the harbor session](../../../research/authored-scenarios/harbor-session.md).

One player tends a dark harbor beacon. The opening offers three quiet opportunities: repair the light (A), secure the tools (B), and wait (W). An apple may already be among the character's things. Its presence does not create an "eat the apple" choice. The numbers below are this fixture's authored target. They are not a universal timing rule or a universal reward.

| Step | Authored target | What it must not mean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H0 | The player sees A, B, and W, and explicitly selects A. Work begins. Ordinary ticks do not call a model. | Starting the repair is not a generation, and possession is not a new action. |
| Tick 10 | A declared occurrence interrupts A at its actual 3/9 contribution. A background tab or a closed browser cannot add work past that hold. A guessed future completion is not history. | Leaving the page does not finish the repair. |
| H1–H3 | Three short Storyteller-directed decisions happen at that same world tick. A, B, and W do not return while that control is active. Only the offered choices and their supported checks apply. Recognizing a genuine badge is not knowledge of a secret the scene did not give. | A menu refresh does not resume the repair. |
| H4 | After the interruption is resolved, the new passage explicitly offers A, B, and W again. It does not silently resume A. | Clearing the interruption is not the same as choosing the repair. |
| B, then the same A | The player finishes B, then resumes the same A. A finishes at tick 25 and grants four credits once. | A second completion does not pay those credits again. |
| W | W proceeds only after the player selects it, and it reaches tick 35. | A completion report does not start the wait. |
| Delayed report | The optional report is history. It is not the current choice, and it does not grant the credits again. | An old notification is not a new action. |
| Return | Re-entry should show the same now, history, progress, and offer, without a duplicate action. | This page does not prove that return. |

A co-op partner, a transformed body, a timed delegated fallback, a complete map, and proprietary world content are not required for this solo proof. They are not cancelled. Calling the prose "green," or counting a synthetic receipt, is not human acceptance. Provider-connected dialogue quality is unproved. A restart that shows the real published now and history is unproved here.

## 1. Arrive and create

**Stage:** solo V0 for arrival, sign-in, and creation. The invited friend is beyond V0. **Missing:** a join protocol. **Failure:** treating the invitation as required before the first solo story. **Provider shape (illustrative):** at most one optional preview in the shared vignette; none is mandatory for the solo target, and no shared-decision loop.

A visitor sees an example of a character's life interrupted by a choice. They select “New story,” sign in through OAuth and reach creation. A returning player instead finds their existing stories and the same creation action. An empty account should not require navigating an empty dashboard first.

The player writes: “I am a wizard crossing the mountains to reach a haunted house.” They choose a whimsical but dangerous storytelling direction and a pace suitable for occasional attention during the day. The premise could equally support horror or gentle comedy; selecting this character does not determine the storyteller's style.

**Historical shared vignette (conditional).** If the player invites a friend, who establishes a companion for the journey, both see the premise and shared expectations for pace and unattended choices. In this vignette a generated preview introduces the pair, their immediate surroundings and a reason to reach the house, and they correct any misunderstanding before starting. That preview and correction example is conditional on the shared vignette. It is not the selected first solo target, which has **no mandatory generated preview** and no separate Start approval: [Entry and onboarding](../experience/entry-and-onboarding.md#premise-stale-previews-and-captured-openings) and the vision's [first playable target](../foundations/vision.md#first-playable-target) go straight from Play or Story Prompt to the first scene. That invitation illustrates the distant small-party aspiration. It is outside the solo [First playable target](../foundations/vision.md#first-playable-target). Its live acceptance is Proposed/Blocked until shared decision rules are ratified. In scope here: arrival, sign-in and creation as [Creation information architecture](../experience/entry-and-onboarding.md#creation-information-architecture) already states them. Out of scope: a join protocol or a way to collect conflicting intentions. Those stay [proposed shared play](../../../work/designs/backlog/shared-play/README.md).

## 2. Set out

**Stage:** solo V0 when one character takes the pass. The joint decision is beyond V0. **Missing:** a way to collect two intentions. **Failure:** inferring an implemented co-op resolver from the agreement. **Provider shape (illustrative):** no model call for the walk itself.

The opening presents the mountain path and several possible intentions. The party decides to take the pass. How that joint decision is collected is still open; for this example they agree, so there is no conflict to resolve. The agreement is fiction inside the small-party ambition. The collection method stays [proposed shared play](../../../work/designs/backlog/shared-play/README.md).

The scene changes to the journey. A background illustration may show the travelers, while the main text describes their movement and estimated arrival. There is no need to refresh the image or generate prose every minute. The players can leave the page.

The story may already have prepared an interruption. Nothing about that preparation belongs in the visible chronology. If the party changes route before reaching it, the old continuation must not fire blindly. In scope: [From intention to continuation](actions-and-commitments.md#from-intention-to-continuation), where a planned arrival is not a completed journey. This scene does not add a route-planning system.

## 3. Meet the goblin

**Stage:** solo V0 illustration of one choice and an immediate result. The companion's response is beyond V0. **Missing:** no selected goblin ruleset. **Failure:** reading the scene as an executed test or as delegated-decision policy. **Provider shape (illustrative):** one consequence narration.

Partway through the journey, a notification brings the players back. A goblin blocks the path, offering an apple and a pear. The view makes clear that a response is available and, if applicable, when a default will be selected.

The wizard's options might be to accept either fruit, tell a joke, wait or attack. These are different intentions, not a mandated five-button template. The companion may also have a role, but the wizard's player cannot choose the companion's response.

The wizard chooses the apple. A short processing state acknowledges the choice, then the consequence follows promptly: the wizard is tiny, inside the apple beside a seed. There is no artificial two-hour delay between accepting the fruit and seeing this immediate result.

Scope: the goblin, the fruit and any response clock in this scene are illustrative fiction. They do not select a delegated default. The governing prohibitions are [What it must not do](../storyteller/behavior-and-quality.md#what-it-must-not-do) and [Decisions with deadlines](../../technical/engine/chronology-and-activities.md#decisions-with-deadlines): do not import the narrative-rehearsal deadline into the mechanical campaign. [First playable scope](time-and-autonomy.md#first-playable-scope) leaves an ordinary unanswered decision open. A general timed fallback remains proposed. The archived [GM07](../../../work/changes/archive/2026-11/experience/2026-11-13--gm-delegated-decision/CHANGE.md) packet covers one harmless invitation and is not selected policy. The landed rows [Implemented decision deadline](../../technical/platform/data-and-persistence.md#implemented-decision-deadline) and [Implemented solo response deadline](../../technical/platform/execution-and-delivery.md#implemented-solo-response-deadline) are a source exception for that fixture. They are not this engine policy. The companion's own response belongs to the small-party ambition. Choosing another player's action is outside this scene.

## 4. Wait inside the apple

**Stage:** solo V0 illustration of an accepted wait. A companion cutting the apple is beyond V0. **Missing:** no shared-object rule. **Failure:** scheduling uninterrupted hours while another character can destroy the apple. **Provider shape (illustrative):** a later notification, not prose every minute.

The wizard chooses to wait and observe. The presentation settles into darkness around the seed and explains that time is passing. This does not require fresh narration while nothing changes.

In the solo version, the goblin might carry the apple away. In the shared version, the companion is still outside and may act. We cannot truthfully schedule five hours of uninterrupted waiting if the companion picks up, cuts open or destroys the apple in the meantime. Their action would change both characters' circumstances.

For this branch, assume nobody intervenes and the apple stays with the goblin. Several real hours later, a notification reports that the goblin is about to eat it. The wizard now has a response window and contextual ways to attempt escape. The story must remember who has the apple, who is inside and what capabilities remain plausible. Who holds the apple is [Six kinds of record](continuity.md#six-kinds-of-record) once later correctness depends on that identity. The wizard's changed form uses [Flexible settings and protagonists](worlds-and-characters.md#flexible-settings-and-protagonists): abilities apply to the current form, and a missing humanoid inventory is not invented here. The hours and the eating clock are illustration, not a selected deadline.

## 5. Respond or miss the window

**Stage:** solo V0 constraint that a missed window invents no choice. Simultaneous rescue is beyond V0. **Missing:** the consent matrix is still proposed. **Failure:** both a rescue and a swallow commit. **Provider shape (illustrative):** none until an authorized intention exists.

If the wizard's player responds in time, resolve that intention. If they do not, the story must not invent a choice the player did not authorize. That prohibition is [What it must not do](../storyteller/behavior-and-quality.md#what-it-must-not-do). A general delegated choice is not accepted. Whether a pre-authorized fallback may answer is Proposed/Blocked with the shared-play acceptance boundary in [First playable scope](time-and-autonomy.md#first-playable-scope). A late response cannot replace an outcome already applied.

The precise handling of a companion's simultaneous rescue attempt needs a shared decision rule. It must not accidentally produce both a successful rescue and the wizard being swallowed because two continuations ran independently. That rule is still [proposed shared play](../../../work/designs/backlog/shared-play/README.md). The rescue itself stays inside the small-party ambition. Simultaneous consent cases stay Proposed in [the consent and resolution matrix](../../../work/designs/backlog/shared-play/README.md#consent-and-resolution-matrix).

If the story is explicitly paused before resolution, its countdown stops, under [Pause and resume](time-and-autonomy.md#pause-and-resume). Group permission to do this remains to be chosen. Closing a browser alone does not pause the story. [Commands, recovery and what the player sees](../../technical/engine/authority-and-transitions.md#commands-recovery-and-what-the-player-sees) gives a closed UI no new permission.

## 6. Return and conclude

**Stage:** solo V0 return to the current situation. A companion's overnight action is beyond V0. **Missing:** no shared recap rule. **Failure:** an old notification reopens a closed choice. **Provider shape (illustrative):** one return account, not a new adventure system.

The next morning, a player sees the current situation and a short account of what happened during their absence, as [After absence](../experience/return-and-journal.md#after-absence) requires. An autonomous choice appears in that account only when it was specifically pre-authorized. Whether that account includes a fallback the player already authorized is part of the Proposed/Blocked shared-play boundary. The account includes other meaningful changes. They can inspect the full chronology if interested. An old phone notification opens this current state rather than reviving the old choice.

An eventual conclusion could be escaping the goblin and reaching the house, abandoning the journey or dying during the attempt. The storyteller should connect the conclusion to what happened. Whether the survivors can start another adventure as the same characters is an open product choice, not an assumed continuation feature.

## What the first playable build should demonstrate

The concrete solo story is [Harbor and return](#first-solo-acceptance-walkthrough--harbor-and-return). This heading remains the short obligation list. The first tested playability milestone is solo: creation, one scene, a quiet interval the player already accepted, an immediate consequence, and a return after absence. The harbor numbers are that story's authored target, not a second policy. A small group in one story is a distant later aspiration, not this demonstration. The exact must-pass acceptance for live small-group play stays Proposed/Blocked in [First playable target](../foundations/vision.md#first-playable-target). It is not cancelled and not implemented. Shared decisions stay [proposed](../../../work/designs/backlog/shared-play/README.md). A general delegated choice stays proposed in [First playable scope](time-and-autonomy.md#first-playable-scope). The archived packet is [GM07](../../../work/changes/archive/2026-11/experience/2026-11-13--gm-delegated-decision/CHANGE.md), and it is not a selected rule. A scripted shared scene does not authorize a response-window policy. Closing the browser does not authorize a new choice; that withholding is [Commands, recovery and what the player sees](../../technical/engine/authority-and-transitions.md#commands-recovery-and-what-the-player-sees). Verify that a change of intention invalidates an incompatible prepared development ([From intention to continuation](actions-and-commitments.md#from-intention-to-continuation)), that a notification opens the current story ([After absence](../experience/return-and-journal.md#after-absence)), and that progression survives pause and restart ([Pause and resume](time-and-autonomy.md#pause-and-resume)).

Measure inference during both waiting and active exchanges. Show that a remembered fact changes a later outcome. This makes orchestration, persistence and efficient AI visible through an engaging experience rather than requiring a large simulated world.
