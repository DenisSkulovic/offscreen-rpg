# Worlds and characters | flexible forms, sparse identity

**Target invariant:** a story does not need a humanoid protagonist or even a conventional “character.” The playable perspective may be personal, collective, microscopic, geographic, observational, distributed or abstract. A prepared experience may constrain that perspective; a custom Story Prompt may establish one through admitted fiction.

**Owns:** what kinds of protagonists and settings the product must support, and when identity becomes durable.  
**Companions:** [continuity](continuity.md), [concepts](../foundations/concepts.md), [rules](rules.md).

## Flexible settings and protagonists

Canonical minimum: the core must not require one profession, one human body, one economy, or one geography. The current playable target is one authored setting and the narrow rules subset. Unsupported: six abilities on every creature, a universal profession system, and a claim that arbitrary worlds are already enjoyable. Unratified alternatives and world-neutral counterexamples: [domain coverage research](../../../research/product-reality/DOMAIN-COVERAGE-2026-11-18.md). Failure gate: a microorganism or a bodiless mind receives a humanoid inventory because a human character has one.

Settings and characters should be flexible: fantasy, space, everyday life, unusual creatures or mixtures. The core must not require a specific profession, human body, economy or geography. Creative freedom still needs continuity: established facts and player choices have weight.

The selected D&D rules can remain richer than a particular character’s current form. Applicability belongs to the character and situation: a bodiless consciousness may have no current use for Strength; a later embodied form can gain abilities through an admitted transformation. Preserve conventional D&D behavior when the world supports it; do not flatten rules to the smallest common denominator or pretend every rule applies to every form. That limit is [World-independent foundation](rules.md#world-independent-foundation). [World-neutral capability kernel](../../technical/engine/selected-architecture.md#world-neutral-capability-kernel) is the selected conceptual target, not a schema and not a source-fixture policy.

**Negative:** do not invent Farmer Bob because farms exist beyond the hill. Do not build one LLM agent per NPC, place or faction. Do not give a microorganism or a disembodied mind a humanoid inventory because a human character has one. A described person is not a canonical actor until later correctness depends on that identity. See [Six kinds of record](continuity.md#six-kinds-of-record).

## Sparse world, three layers

1. **Implied / background** — atmosphere and population that make the setting feel real but have never become individually relevant. No durable rows merely because such things logically exist.
2. **Materialized scene detail** — people, objects and environment actually presented in a passage. Appearance alone does not require a full persistent world entity.
3. **Durable continuity entities** — identity earned when later correctness depends on it (engagement, ownership/location/relationship change, deliberate revisit, or future rules need that exact thing). Absence from current context is not deletion.

Representation of durable people, places and objects is chosen when a concrete gameplay slice needs it—not as a census of unused entities.

## Starts and pinned copies

An authored start may assemble world, rules, characters, relationships, known threads, private possibilities and executable obligations into one reusable starting position. Only content actually present in that start exists. Expected future plots remain possibilities unless play establishes them; scheduled requirements use validated obligations. Starting a campaign makes an independent pinned copy of campaign-owned material; later edits to the reusable start do not rewrite a story already in progress.

## Shared play (product target, unsettled mechanics)

The long-term experience still names inviting friends into a shared story, each controlling their own character. The first tested playability milestone is solo, as [First playable target](../foundations/vision.md#first-playable-target) states. The small group is a distant later aspiration. It is not cancelled and not implemented. The exact live acceptance for shared decision and pause rules is Proposed/Blocked. Do not treat a UI prototype as acceptance of those rules.
