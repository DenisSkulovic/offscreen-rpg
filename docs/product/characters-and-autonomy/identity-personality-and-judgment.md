# Identity, personality and judgment

Owner: Denis. Release disposition: unassigned.
Basis: [terms](../foundations/glossary-and-domain-map.md), [principles](../foundations/product-principles.md).

## Direction and status

Previously confirmed: autonomous judgment may be foolish because of the character's intelligence, personality and flaws (D020).

New user-proposed direction: the character should also constrain direct player control. Taking over should not grant the player's own knowledge, skills or unrestricted freedom to behave as a different person. The specific meaning and strength of personality restrictions remain open.

The grumpy fisherman is a brainstorming example, not a required character, class, profession system or starting scenario. References to other games are analogies, not adopted mechanics.

## Different reasons an action may be limited

| Dimension | Question to resolve | What not to assume |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mechanical capability | Does the adopted D&D ruleset allow the attempt, and with what uncertainty? | Low skill always means an action is forbidden |
| Knowledge and belief | What can the character know or reasonably infer? | The player's external knowledge automatically belongs to the character |
| Personality and motivation | Is the choice plausible for this person in these circumstances? | A single adjective completely determines every decision |
| Physical and situational constraints | Can the action occur with current resources and circumstances? | Narrative confidence supplies missing equipment or abilities |

These distinctions are proposed design vocabulary, not new attribute systems. Adopted D&D mechanics must be checked before defining additional personality restrictions as adaptations.

## Illustrative cases

A fisherman may attempt to bargain without becoming an expert diplomat. A grumpy character might help someone reluctantly. An unfamiliar technical solution described by the player may depend on knowledge the character lacks.

These examples test the boundary; they do not choose success probabilities, hidden checks, dialogue menus or mandatory costs. Profession and personality are not shorthand for intelligence. Nor does low intelligence imply that every choice is irrational.

## Options and forks

All candidates; release disposition unassigned.

| Fork | Alternatives and tradeoffs | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IDENTITY-F01: personality constraint | Some choices unavailable; choices allowed with a cost or resistance; character-shaped interpretation of intent; combinations by trait/context | Compare an implausible choice with an unusual but believable one |
| IDENTITY-F02: explaining limits | Brief reason and nearby alternatives; reveal relevant trait/rule details; minimal explanation under the chosen style | Determine what helps the player understand the character without exposing unwanted mechanics |
| IDENTITY-F03: acting against disposition | Never for some boundaries; allowed under strong motive; possible through change over time | Define whether and how character development changes options |
| IDENTITY-F04: direct versus autonomous expression | Both use the same constraints; direct control can influence choices more strongly while retaining capability limits | Reconcile player agency with the character's independent identity |

No stress meter, obedience score, new dice check or fixed personality taxonomy has been selected. A repeated refusal based on a vague adjective would require a defined product rationale; it should not become an unexplained narrator veto.

## Relationships to other topics

[Knowledge and beliefs](knowledge-beliefs-and-secrets.md) owns information available to the character. Rules documents own capabilities and resolution. This document will own how personality and identity shape choices.

Open questions: what makes a choice implausible; how player intent is interpreted; what identity facts can change; detailed interactions between instructions and motives (see [goals/orders](goals-orders-and-permissions.md)); which limits are configuration choices. Preserve these alternatives until a concrete play example or rules baseline lets us evaluate them.

## Current recommendation for the first slice

Keep autonomous fallibility in the first slice; park strong personality-based vetoes under direct control until concrete cases justify them. This recommendation is owned for release placement by DOC-RELEASE-SCOPE and does not cancel the user's proposed identity constraint. In the meantime, do not use a vague adjective as an unlimited narrator veto. Capability and information restrictions have separate rule owners. IDENTITY-F01 remains the material product choice.
