# Goals, orders and permissions

Owner: Denis. Release disposition: unassigned.
Sources: [identity](identity-personality-and-judgment.md), [knowledge](knowledge-beliefs-and-secrets.md), [time/autonomy](../experience/time-presence-and-autonomy.md).

## Confirmed direction: a plan is not guaranteed execution

Denis explicitly wants autonomous characters to be able to abandon or drift from player plans because of who they are. They can intend to work and later spend the time elsewhere. The player may discover this on returning after days of absence.

This includes failing to follow one's own intention, not only misunderstanding an order, choosing a poor tactic or encountering an external obstacle. The character is not a reliable task executor.

Drinking instead of working is an illustration, not a required addiction system, preset trait, scripted event or inevitable outcome.

## Distinctions that matter

| Concept | Meaning here |
| --- | --- |
| Player plan | What the player wants the character to pursue |
| Character intention | What the character is currently trying or intending to do |
| Actual behavior | What the character does as time and circumstances unfold |
| Result | What follows; even faithful effort can fail |
| Campaign restriction | A rule governing permissible play, distinct from an in-fiction intention |

A character can initially share the player's plan and subsequently abandon it. Conversely, doing something unexpected might still advance the goal. Neither case should be judged solely by whether the final reward was obtained.

## Draft behavior

| ID | Proposed expectation |
| --- | --- |
| GOAL-001 | Giving an autonomous character a plan must not imply guaranteed adherence or successful completion. |
| GOAL-002 | Allow a character's motivation and circumstances to lead to postponement, deviation or abandonment while the world runs under the chosen autonomy policy. |
| GOAL-003 | Distinguish a planned activity from activity actually performed when reporting progress and results. |
| GOAL-004 | Apply rewards, costs and consequences to resolved behavior; an unperformed job does not earn wages merely because it was planned. |
| GOAL-005 | Character disobedience must not bypass global pause, locked campaign settings or other actual game restrictions. |

Fictional judgment may be poor. The distinction between hard campaign restrictions and instructions the character can disregard must be explicit when those controls are designed.

## Illustrative progression

The player plans several days of work. The character begins, then a competing desire or opportunity draws them elsewhere. They may return to the plan, neglect it entirely, or produce a different outcome. On returning, the player discovers what happened.

The product should not invent a moral lesson or insist that deviation always ends badly. Nor should it reward perfect adherence merely because the player wrote a detailed plan. The character's traits, circumstances and configured autonomy matter.

The return experience is owned by [leaving/returning](../experience/leaving-and-returning.md), especially RETURN-003/004. Whether a deviation itself triggers a notification remains open.

## Plans meet a changing world

Confirmed clarification from Denis: divergence is not limited to personality or disobedience. External events can interrupt, invalidate or transform a plan, and the character then responds from the resulting situation. A week of intended work is not a reserved week of guaranteed employment, access, health or safety.

Distinguish: abandoning an available plan; being prevented from carrying it out; adapting after circumstances change; and combinations of these over time. Work may stop because its workplace becomes unavailable, after which the character may seek other work, remain idle, or pursue something else according to their circumstances and judgment. Neither adaptation nor its success is guaranteed.

The player returns to the history that actually unfolded. Progress and summaries must not infer completion from the original plan or blame every departure on personality. Multiple developments can compound: an interruption changes location, resources, obligations or health, which changes the next available choices.

Mining, war, closure, conscription, infestation and illness are user illustrations of this principle, not a mandatory event catalogue or approved simulation subsystems. The range of events, their frequency, and first-release coverage remain open. All progression still obeys campaign pause, pace and autonomy policies.

World developments are owned by [world events and causality](../worlds/world-events-and-causality.md). This topic owns how they affect the relevance and pursuit of an existing plan.

## Options and forks

All candidates; release disposition unassigned.

| Fork | Alternatives and tradeoffs | What would help choose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GOAL-F01: expression of intent | Broad goals; scheduled activities; behavioral preferences; a combination | Compare how a player expresses an ordinary week versus a specific expedition |
| GOAL-F02: enforceability | Intention only; distinguish instructions from hard permitted-action limits; configurable influence | Clarify what the player is truly controlling versus asking the character to do |
| GOAL-F03: deviation visibility | Learn on return; receive selected notices; observe live when present | Balance surprise against the desired opportunity to intervene |
| GOAL-F04: reasons shown | Observable history; character explanations that may be unreliable; selected mechanical explanation | Decide the desired insight into a character's motives |
| GOAL-F05: revising a plan | Replace previous directions; reprioritize; negotiate conflict with current intention | Define how new instructions interact with traits and ongoing activity |

These are not predefined game modes. No fixed compliance chance, needs hierarchy, personality score or new D&D mechanic is selected.

## Boundaries

[Identity](identity-personality-and-judgment.md) owns the character traits and their influence; this topic owns the relationship between direction, intention and behavior. Knowledge owns information available to the character. Handoffs own when control transfers. [Routines/work/needs](routines-work-and-needs.md) describes ongoing activities; [autonomous risk](autonomous-risk-and-decisions.md) distinguishes choices from their outcomes.

Control settings may change the experience, but exact obedience options are unresolved. The long-term product must retain the ability for a character to stray from a plan; do not quietly return to guaranteed autonomous execution.

Still open: how much direct influence the player retains after delegation, what can be absolutely prohibited, how motivations change, and what the player can learn about why the character acted. No commitment to a particular implementation or first-release scope follows from this draft.
