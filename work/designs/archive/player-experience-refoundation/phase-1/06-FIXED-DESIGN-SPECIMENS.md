# Phase 1 | Fixed narrative specimens for visual and interaction critique

**Status:** OPEN DESIGN. Everything in this file is **invented for design comparison**, not a source passage or a recorded campaign result. Later mockups must label it as fixture content and preserve the copy across competing directions.

## Why fixed content is necessary

If we compare a beautiful painting of an exciting dragon scene to a dull “inventory” mockup, we are judging **stories**, not interfaces. Phase 2 art directions must render **the same exact narratives, states and controls**. There must be at least one case with *no artwork*, one nonhuman world and one mundane long-duration commitment. A design that only looks compelling with a hero illustration is not a robust narrative platform.

## Specimen A — The beacon and the visitor

**Purpose:** test contemporary/literary prose hierarchy, a continuing scene across multiple passages, meaningful authored intentions, a long undertaking and a source-linked old moment.

**World:** small coastal outpost, pre-industrial but without mandatory parchment/medieval brand assets.
**Current location:** an exterior signal beacon, if available from the fixture's authored world context.
**Published passage A-184 — original mock prose:**

> **The Lamp Above Greywater**
>
> By the third hour, the wind had managed to pry one of the brass plates from its bolts. Mara caught it against her coat before it slipped into the dark.
>
> The road below the beacon was empty, though the bell at the ferry house had rung twice. She put the plate on the workbench and listened. Someone was climbing the steps.
>
> The stranger stopped outside the doorway. His right sleeve was soaked through, but the canvas parcel under his arm was dry.

**Authored decision prompt:** “He waits for you to speak. What do you do?”

**Choice A (intent):** “Ask who sent him.” Clarifier: a brief question that may keep the encounter civil.

**Choice B (intent):** “Look at the parcel before speaking.” Clarifier: inspection may take time and be noticed.

**Choice C (intent):** “Return to the lamp.” Clarifier: deliberately decline the encounter only if the fixture's offer truly authorizes it.

These are **not** guaranteed outcomes or promises of success. Exactly how much fictional time an action takes is admission-specific; none is implied by this decorative fixture.

**Adjacent published passage A-185 — original mock prose:**

> **The Seal**
>
> The stranger lowered the parcel onto the bench. Its wax was blue, stamped with a bird whose wings were turned inward.
>
> Mara knew that mark. Years earlier she had carved the same shape on a piece of driftwood and left it with a child who had promised to return.

This second passage is **not necessarily a different scene**. It makes the archive's source-backed relevance and an early ordinary gift concrete. A later editorial overview may link to the earlier gift if it can cite the original passage. Without that source, it can only present the current words and offer ordinary text search.

**Candidate source-faithful Journal teaser:**
- Excerpt (original): “The stranger lowered the parcel onto the bench.”
- Editorial gist (derived, explicitly marked): “A familiar seal stirs an older memory.” This is **not** quoted original prose.
- Source: “Passage A-185” (fixture identity; real product would link true authorized passage ID).

## Specimen B — Accepted work without a dramatic update

**Purpose:** test restrained ongoing state, when a meaningful activity has begun but completion has not been earned.

**Prose (mock published passage):**

> **The Third Plate**
>
> The bent plate would hold for tonight. The others needed replacing, but a hand could only drive so many rivets before the knuckles stiffened.
>
> Mara set the first tool against the seam. There was enough daylight left to work.

**Admitted fixture state (hypothetical):** active repair, 2 of 5 verified units earned, completion not admitted, checkpoint due only if source model provides one, Pause available only if this exact fixture permits it.

**Do not mock as:** “100% repaired” from a local timer, a spinning glowing “world evolving” progress bar, or a forced choice just because blank space looks empty.

**Return variant (A):** no new committed development. UI honestly shows the same active work and last published prose.  
**Return variant (B):** a recorded interruption, with a sourced new passage and option.  
**Failure variant:** mechanical settlement committed, prose preparation uncertain; the UI exposes the saved result and a safe recovery path instead of implying a reroll.

Designs should present all variants in the same product identity while allowing differing amounts of atmospheric motion.

## Specimen C — A self without a face, place or equipment grid

**Purpose:** prove platform neutrality and prevent all visual thinking collapsing into fantasy art.

**World:** a consciousness distributed across synchronized vibrations in the rings of a gas giant. No body, face, inventory, street, city, conventional map or day/night cycle is required.

**Published passage C-007 — original mock prose:**

> **Interference**
>
> For the first time in forty rotations, the seventh current does not return your call.
>
> You feel the absence where a familiar harmony once crossed the gap. A weaker pattern arrives instead, repeating a fragment that belonged to no member of the chorus.
>
> Something has entered the space between the rings. It is patient, and it is listening.

**Intentions:** “Answer the unfamiliar pattern”; “Hold your signal and observe”; “Withdraw from the chorus” **only if independently admitted as a valid option**.

**Visual demands:** compose presence through typography, spacing, soundless waveform metaphor or bounded motion. Do not fall back to an unrelated astronaut image. A reader must see the choice, passage and source history without a map or character portrait.

**Archive variant:** nineteen passages of signal exchanges represent one continuing perceptual scene. Grouping should not force a new scene per passage.

## Specimen D — A mundane life without gamification

**Purpose:** prove that not every moment needs an artificial dramatic hook.

**Published passage D-042 — original mock prose:**

> **The Last Bus**
>
> The route was empty past the hospital. Ilse kept the heater low and watched the reflections of the streetlights pass along the windshield.
>
> At the terminus, she found a receipt folded into a paper crane. No message, no signature. She put it beside the route book and finished checking the seats.

**State:** no current choice; no known active execution. This is an acceptable quiet situation.

**Testing aim:** can the design feel deliberate and alive without a large CTA, fake activity timer, AI-generated dramatic label, or permanent progress dashboard? Can an ordinary found object become easy to find in history later without asserting it is a durable canonical inventory record?
