## Remaining material finding
### RMQ-C06 — Medium/high: durable fixture orchestration lacks a frozen custody and fault-attribution boundary
FINAL-TARGET §4 specifies run-scoped Pods/ConfigMaps and private cleanup receipt. R9 requires exact server-generated queue name persisted before publish, then distinct publisher/hold/replacement phases and a host supervisor deleting the worker. Existing infra/verification/faults/run.sh apply_client/wait_succeeded/hold loop orchestrates separate Pods via logs; worker scripts have no mounted host receipt and no SA token. R9 freezes the wire protocol but does not say which process owns durable receipt persistence, how a queue declared inside a Pod crosses to that owner before publish, or how publishing waits for proof of that persistence. A private receipt written inside an ephemeral publisher Pod can disappear before the supervisor learns the queue name; logging a name before publish is not a host persistence acknowledgement. A process crash between declaration and receipt is legitimately ambiguous and may retain a queue, but a successful publication must not rely on an ephemeral-only receipt.
Additionally, observing APPLY then deleting a Pod and receiving redelivered=1 does not by itself establish that the injected worker loss caused the redelivery: the hold connection can fail after APPLY and before deletion. The R9 hold deadline rule excludes one cause but not heartbeat/socket failure or already terminated worker. Freeze the observable fault attribution and its limitations, including a failure result if independent pre-delete hold failure is observed, or explicitly narrow the claim to observed unacked redelivery after a worker-loss exercise rather than causal attribution. Do not demand impossible atomic observation of AMQP and Kubernetes.
Closure: specify receipt owner, cross-process transport/order and acknowledgement (or a single coherent owner boundary), fail/retain behavior at every declaration/receipt/publish interruption, and bounded host orchestration deadlines including pod scheduling, log observation, termination, replacement and cleanup. Include deterministic negative traces for receipt loss before publish, publisher crash, hold failure before deletion, timeout and identity mismatch. Existing R9 protocol and cleanup rules otherwise stand. Exact commands may be projected into a later packet; the ownership and order must not be delegated as architecture choices. No Pika adoption or generic orchestration framework required.
