# Editorial audit | canonical product and technical content

**Source basis:** supplied lean ZIP at f3e3bfe35490932ee363c00aad0fb239893d825e. Current GitHub comparison through f5c96645232ac746c67d4cabb273300986895e48 found no changes to docs/product/** or docs/technical/**; other work was concurrent. Corpus-wide inventory, heading/first-screen reading, phrase search and selected deep reads were performed. This is **not** a claim that every paragraph of all 70 substantive pages has yet been independently semantically verified, or that current code/tests were rerun.

## Scope and measurements

- **73** Markdown files in docs/product and docs/technical, including three README hubs; **70** substantive pages.
- About **129,339** whitespace-separated words and **7,041** lines across those 73 pages. The technical tree accounts for about **99,154** words, product for about **30,185**.
- **25** pages exceed 2,000 words. Largest: storyteller/context-and-memory.md (~8.3k words / 341 lines), storyteller/request-context-and-cost.md (~6.9k / 226), storyteller/operation-and-controller.md (~5.2k / 131), platform/data-and-persistence.md (~5.1k / 202), client/architecture.md (~4.4k / 261).
- The preceding [link audit](../documentation-impact-system/AUDIT-2026-11-18.md) measured 35 of 70 pages with no other product/technical cross-document link beyond line 20. This is only a *navigation signal*, not proof the prose is defective.
- Exact long-paragraph duplication was **not** prevalent: a normalized paragraph scan found zero long exact paragraphs repeated across files. The main problem is semantic overlap, lifecycle mixing and buried authority, **not** mass copy-paste.

## Findings ranked by severity

### F01 — Critical editorial ambiguity: benchmark vs ratified first playable

[Gameplay acceptance scenarios](../../../../docs/product/gameplay/acceptance-scenarios.md) says the six-step goblin story is illustrative and shared action resolution unsettled. Nevertheless, **“What the first playable build should demonstrate”** demands “a shared scene” and “a timed autonomous choice.” [Vision](../../../../docs/product/foundations/vision.md) says shared decisions and pause are unresolved, and general [delegated decision](../../../changes/open/2026-11-13--gm-delegated-decision/CHANGE.md) is Proposed. That combination can make an implementation agent mistake a benchmark for accepted near-term scope.

**Treatment:** distinguish ratified solo acceptance, aspirational group/delegation stress tests, and explicit policy-decision gates. Do not delete the goblin or long-life benchmarks. Preserve the imaginative user experience while making test authority legible. The exact first-playable scope is an owner decision; do not secretly settle it by editing the example.

### F02 — Actual current-implementation-status disagreement in technical texts

[Authority and transitions](../../../../docs/technical/engine/authority-and-transitions.md) opens with “execution-permission and short-action correction” **prepared but not implemented**, while [Chronology and activities](../../../../docs/technical/engine/chronology-and-activities.md) opens with execution gate and finite-action boundary **implemented** with offline tests. Both describe overlapping time/execution behavior, although not necessarily identical sub-slices.

**Treatment:** inspect exact source, accepted packet receipts and targeted tests. Reconcile each sub-slice in terms of implemented/proved, proposed/not built, and blocked. Then repair stale claims *without* rewriting the selected no-unaccepted-time product rule. This is a concrete cross-text inconsistency candidate, not code-proof of which paragraph is correct.

### F03 — Normative/realization/history mixing creates unstable evergreen owners

Examples:
- [Frontend architecture](../../../../docs/technical/client/architecture.md) includes a W01–W22 completion inventory before its decided stack and invariants.
- [Data and persistence](../../../../docs/technical/platform/data-and-persistence.md) begins with 10+ “Implemented ...” subheadings followed by “Planned story records” in the same durable contract.
- [Storyteller context and memory](../../../../docs/technical/storyteller/context-and-memory.md) combines current source shapes, target ownership, implementation version chronology and future agent cycle.
- [Request context and cost](../../../../docs/technical/storyteller/request-context-and-cost.md) covers policy, request construction, retrieval/summaries, caching, budget accounting and cost estimates with intersecting owners.

**Treatment:** keep compatibility/version semantics that matter to future readers; move dated completion ledgers, ephemeral test receipts and next-step checklists to STATUS, source maps, runbooks, evidence or archive by their actual authority. Use a concise “Realization boundary” pointer in a canonical contract when essential. Never eliminate a negative invariant just to shorten a page.

### F04 — Page responsibility incohesion; two documents sometimes pasted into one

[Acceptance scenarios](../../../../docs/product/gameplay/acceptance-scenarios.md) has a second H1, “Long-life benchmark playthroughs,” after the six-step goblin playthrough. They exercise different acceptance modes (short POC sequence vs accumulated earned life). Split could be warranted, with stable links, retained scenario text and a purpose-specific router.

[Context and memory](../../../../docs/technical/storyteller/context-and-memory.md) includes an additional H1, “The visitor at the beacon,” inside a sample content block; do not automatically treat that as a malformed heading until checking its fenced/example semantics. The *actual* cohesion problem is that storage schema, world-package import, memory/summaries, retrieval, agent cycle, publication and recovery reside under one owner. Consider separately addressed contracts by revision/authority boundaries after mapping existing source links.

**Treatment:** split only when consumer, authority, revision cadence and test proof differ. Preserve inbound anchors via compatibility redirects/routes or an intentional link migration in the same editorial commit.

### F05 — Product foundations have durable insight plus avoidable implementation status

[Vision](../../../../docs/product/foundations/vision.md) eloquently states earned life, D&D grounding, creative neutrality and world neutrality. It also embeds portfolio objectives, rough sponsored cost aspirations, and contemporaneous state of shared-play policy. It repeats some of the governing time and mechanics constraints in prose without always identifying ownership on that precise claim.

[Concepts](../../../../docs/product/foundations/concepts.md) is useful, especially turn/scene/receipt separation, but interleaves durable definitions and current code/result-envelope notes.

**Treatment:** preserve high-level creative vision; route current release/prototype/economic milestones to STATUS/roadmap and exact code conventions to technical source owners. Keep essential crosslinks at the sentences where time, consent and mechanics constrain the vision. Do not reduce the page to marketing fluff.

### F06 — Product behavior is frequently correct in principle but under-evaluated in adversarial situations

[Storyteller behavior and quality](../../../../docs/product/storyteller/behavior-and-quality.md) has a valuable adopted falsifiability matrix. Yet “realize style,” “maintain meaningful intent,” “durable memory,” “creative neutrality” and months-long continuity need explicit multi-scene evaluation cases, including cases where **not generating** is correct. A single pleasant scene does not demonstrate agency or sustained narrative quality. Product policy and technical prompt/tool acceptance must stay separately owned.

**Treatment:** add focused positive and discriminating negative acceptance cases with actual evidence requirements, not more adjectives or a generic “quality” score. Keep the existing strong table as baseline, enrich only untestable clauses.

### F07 — Contextual dependencies missing at consequence-bearing claims

Content has strong top-of-page routes but fewer claim-local crosslinks. Good candidates are [time & autonomy](../../../../docs/product/gameplay/time-and-autonomy.md) → exact implementation readiness, [mechanics](../../../../docs/technical/engine/mechanics-and-capabilities.md) → product authority, [Storyteller prompts](../../../../docs/technical/storyteller/prompts-and-tools.md) → product non-delegation and provider spend, and [scene expression](../../../../docs/technical/client/semantic-design-foundation.md) → visual stability. Dependencies should state *why* a change threatens the dependent, not only list “See also.” Do not impose a link quota.

### F08 — Unratified security/privacy/retention policy must not be edited into authority

[Admin authority](../../../../docs/product/admin/authority-and-governance.md) explicitly labels grant packages proposed. [Retention and access](../../../../docs/technical/observability/retention-and-access.md) describes exploratory/candidate raw AI retention windows and requires legal/product/cost review. [Evidence graph](../../../../docs/technical/observability/identities-and-evidence.md) is a **selected** target but its Turn Capsule section is a **proposed** read contract. An editorial cleanup that removes those qualifications is a security regression.

**Treatment:** keep proposal labels next to the exact unratified tables/sections, rather than one vague disclaimer at the top; never turn a recommendation into production permission through prettier prose.

### F09 — Large documents need a claim-local extraction route, not only an introductory TOC

Good top-level question routers already exist in client architecture, time, context/memory and request/cost. But a 2–8k-word technical article can force an agent to extract invariants from paragraphs that mix problem, decision, implementation detail, alternative and version history. A selected Change needs **the exact relevant heading and falsifying edge case** without the rest of the novel.

**Treatment:** write claim-led technical sections: decision/invariant -> why -> permitted behavior -> forbidden behavior -> example/negative -> exact related dependency; move historical narrative to dedicated evidence/decision where necessary. Keep crucial formulas, schemas and error identity untouched unless separately approved.

### F10 — Lack of explicit semantics for “must / should / may / not yet”

A corpus-wide word scan found ~467 “must”, ~122 “should”, ~449 “may”, ~244 “selected”, ~83 “proposed”, ~87 “implemented”, and ~151 “target” occurrences. Counts are not bad by themselves, but many pages mix chosen invariant, preferred technique, optional capability and proposed future in one article. Adverbial authority labels (“should”, “recommended”, “target”) can obscure which rule a coding agent is actually required to satisfy.

**Treatment:** define a small editorial style for selected/required, proposal, option, aspirational example, current realization and prohibited behavior. The *meaning* must be established by owner decision, not by replacing every “should” with “must.”

## Areas that are stronger than the critique

- Vision's earned-life argument and sparse-world principle are coherent and characteristic.
- Product time/autonomy has non-trivial exact clock, pause and time semantics rather than superficial “continue in background.”
- Storyteller behavior already has an adopted falsifiability matrix.
- Technical engine selected architecture has clear responsibility boundaries and causal spine.
- Client architecture has explicit stack decisions, privacy/race/frozen intent and F1–F15 acceptance map.
- Observability documents are notably careful about historical evidence versus telemetry and about withholding unapproved production access.

These strengths should survive the editorial pass. “Make everything brief” would be destructive.
