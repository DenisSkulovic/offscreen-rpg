# E1 | Product nucleus and knowledge architecture

**Selected design candidate for defense, not canon.** Owner direction is recorded separately from designer recommendations. Current accepted contracts remain in their existing owners until a selected change is ratified and promoted.

## 1. Product direction

Owner direction, 2026-11-28: the game is a Storyteller agent with a Markdown writing workbench and Library, assisted and constrained by real D&D-style mechanics. The Storyteller handles creative interpretation and world development; application code performs mechanical calculations and protected state changes. Inventory exchange must be a structured proposal executed by game authority, not agent edits to two inventory documents. The resulting state should be represented coherently in canonical Markdown. D&D mechanics must fit the actual fictional world and protagonist; an intergalactic blob need not have HP or Agility. Worlds may span arbitrary locations, scale, realms and time, including several fictional timelines. No universal 2D grid. The owner wants to revisit ideas before removing or rewriting them, not discard all existing thought indiscriminately.

Owner clarification, 2026-11-28: D&D is optional and a suggested default experience, ranging from full D&D as a product aspiration through selected mechanics to narrative-only play. Offscreen time-dependent play is likewise optional, from immediate progression through hybrid to real-time-dependent play. The owner accepts Markdown for descriptive knowledge and structured documents for protected mechanics; rendered Markdown and its serialized string representation are distinct from authority.

Recommended synthesis: Offscreen RPG is a persistent, configurable storytelling RPG. The player inhabits a perspective and makes consequential decisions; a Storyteller investigates, invents, writes and revises from durable knowledge; supported mechanics settle exact consequences under game authority; an authoritative publication makes the next story state inspectable and recoverable. No universal medieval-life, humanoid, world map, physics or economic simulation is required. A rich story may use these as relevant content or optional capabilities.

### What Offscreen RPG is

Offscreen RPG is a persistent, configurable storytelling roleplaying game in which a Storyteller guides a continuing fictional life. You can begin with a prepared experience or describe a world and perspective in your own words. You can inhabit a person, an organism, a collective, or a form of existence with no familiar body. The game does not require a quest, a predetermined plot, a class, a character sheet or a particular genre.

The Storyteller explores the Library, works out what happens next, stages its own notes and drafts, and presents meaningful possibilities. It is free to make creative judgments inside the campaign's permissions. Game Authority admits the consequences: applicable rules, dice, resources, time, ownership and player decisions are not whatever the model happens to say. The engine is strict about truth and neutral about genre, drama and creative taste.

D&D is a suggested optional rules family, not a universal foundation or character schema. Play can be narrative-only, use selected compatible mechanics, or aspire to a full D&D experience. The game offers supported configurations rather than claiming that every combination or every D&D rule is executable today. A campaign may use a familiar check, combat or inventory mechanic where the campaign and current form make it meaningful. A character without a body need not have bodily attributes. Where a rule is enabled, the game executes it according to the admitted version; where it is not applicable, the Storyteller does not invent a replacement score. Not every action needs a roll, and not every story needs combat. Neither this principle nor an author's Markdown rule text creates a new executable mechanic.

Real-time-dependent progression is a distinctive optional capability, not a mandatory form of play. A player can progress immediately, choose supported hybrid behavior, or commit to travel, work, recovery or other meaningful activity that takes real elapsed time and return to the outcome. Pace controls real waiting for admitted fictional work; it does not change its results. A story with no executable clock need not acquire precise durations simply to tell a narrative. Existing clock-enabled play retains its admitted fictional durations, ordered boundaries, interruption and pause rules. No new player decision is presumed because the player is absent, and waiting for a model is not fictional work. A player may want a companion-like parallel life at 1:1 pace: an accepted two-day journey can occupy two real days, with an earlier eligible event or interruption. A quiet journey completes once; arrival is not granted on departure. Continuous life beyond an accepted finite commitment is a legitimate later aspiration, not an E1 grant of standing autonomy. E2 must distinguish permission to advance time, permission to resolve unattended consequences, permission to choose player intentions, response holds and provider-spend authorization. Quiet life and immediate conversation are equally legitimate. Completion, interruption and earned consequences must follow the accepted commitment and rules.

The Library holds the story's versioned knowledge and evidence. The Workbench is the Storyteller's private, reconstructable workspace for investigation and drafting, not an independently writable world database. Published history, current admitted facts, attributed claims, private possibilities and derived summaries have different meanings. A later writer can recover the same person, object, promise or consequence without pretending an old draft was history. A historical fork starts from the exact admitted boundary and proceeds independently.

The fictional universe is not required to fit a grid, a planet, a single calendar or a linear narrative. Named places, realms, connections, perspectives and time relationships matter when the story depends on them. A grid or a map can help a particular adventure; it is not the game world's universal representation. The player may encounter parallel histories and time travel. These are fictional capabilities, not permission to rewrite committed execution history, mix state from different forks or claim an unsupported timeline mechanic is implemented.

### Gameplay configuration: independent dimensions

The player chooses the kind of experience, not just a world or protagonist. Mechanical depth and real-time dependence are independent dimensions. Suggested presets can expose common combinations while supported custom selections allow a player to compose compatible capabilities. The following examples describe intended experiences, not implemented preset IDs or a promised arbitrary rules-composition engine.

| Mechanical depth | Immediate | Hybrid | Real-time-dependent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Narrative-only | Interactive fiction on demand | Storytelling with selected waits | Narrative developments gated by admitted elapsed time |
| Selected mechanics | Selected checks or exchanges without real waits | Immediate scenes plus selected timed work | Selected rules plus time-dependent commitments |
| Full D&D aspiration | Tabletop-like play on demand | Checks and combat on demand, journeys or work may take time | Rules-heavy persistent life |

Narrative-only means no compulsory D&D checks, character sheet, combat or quantitative simulation. It does not waive consent, publication, visibility, exact historical recovery or protection of any admitted resources. If a campaign introduces counted or otherwise protected state, it must use a supported authoritative operation or remain purely descriptive; disabling a rules family cannot grant unrestricted writes or permit fabricating mechanical outcomes. A narrative-only campaign with timed activities uses time authority even if it has no D&D rules. Real-time dependence never authorizes the engine to invent a player's decision. No wall-clock waiting requirement should be imposed merely because a fictional story contains a duration.

E1 chooses this product direction, not a configuration schema, preset catalog, dependency solver, rule edition, exact defaults, or mid-campaign toggle policy. Existing pinned versions, locked settings, accepted commitments and supported combinations remain binding. In E2, determine compatibility and prerequisites, temporal mode semantics, and migration/reconfiguration boundaries; reject unsupported combinations rather than silently inventing a fallback. Existing pace semantics remain intact in clock-enabled play. Distinguish optional rules/clock participation from pace changes within a clock-enabled campaign. No claim of current fully narrative-only clockless support or independently selectable mechanics without source proof.

## 4. Decision method and page architecture

Write from the newly accepted target while retaining important guarantees, source provenance and explicit unknowns.

For each affected page, the planner first reads its unique claims, incoming/outgoing dependencies, related selected decisions and relevant code/tests if realization is discussed. Map the meaningful units to their exact destination and decision: retain, rewrite without meaning change, explicitly replace selected meaning, merge/move, defer a useful idea, or retire with rationale. Do not require a row for every sentence; use claim-sized units where loss or meaning changes would matter. Deletion of abandoned material is preferred over a permanent disclaimer or archive copy where no unique evidence or binding guarantee needs preservation. Retain licensed content provenance, security/financial constraints, historical evidence and necessary recovery links; use Git for ordinary historical prose. The owner/planner settles contested decisions, not a rewriting agent.

## 5. Counterexamples that must survive the synthesis

- A narrative-only immediate campaign tells a story without a compulsory sheet, dice, precise clock or real waits; publication, consent, visibility and recovery still apply. A selected timed narrative campaign can wait on admitted time without introducing D&D. A D&D-style instant campaign retains applicable dice, fictional durations and rule outcomes without real waits.
- A hybrid campaign requests only certain activities to take real time: supported captured commitments retain their exact outcomes, pause/interrupt semantics and consent; the intended flexibility does not authorize unimplemented selective scheduling or arbitrary mid-flight changes.
- A disembodied consciousness explores several realms: no mandatory HP, inventory, coordinates or physical body; meaningful established facts still constrain later scenes.
- A human attempts a risky climb: writer judges narrative situation and proposes a supported check; game computes from admitted inputs, stores the draw and outcome, refuses an unavailable ability, and retries do not reroll.
- A sword is purchased: a proposed exchange must validate ownership, currency/resources, rule applicability and consent; both sides settle together or neither does; duplicate request cannot charge twice. Canonical Markdown reflects admitted truth, not independent model edits. This is a target scenario, not a claim of current generalized trade support.
- A journey crosses a portal or branches across fictional eras: no global coordinate system required; do not confuse in-fiction time travel with a player-created historical fork. Preserve meaningful origin/destination, interrupted state, knowledge and causal constraints where applicable; do not design every time-travel paradox in E1.
- The writer drafts a false rumor or future event: staged or attributed text is not an authoritative fact merely because it exists in the workbench. Publication must not expose hidden knowledge or make a planned event historical prematurely.
- The player forks scene 10 after reaching scene 30: the branch inherits only the exact admitted historical state and relevant versioned material at scene 10, never later possession, private developments or current model settings by accident.
