### Book renderer / third-party dependency risk study

The owner rejected treating a flipbook dependency as the foundation of the game, fearing future fights with rigid page sizing, source content, Storyteller-driven visuals and interactivity. Investigated OpenPageFlip (MIT, young, edge-only selectable HTML), Gul Labs Flipbook (MPL-2.0 core, React wrapper; documented engine root styling and clone snapshots), StPageFlip and print-oriented Paged.js. Wrote [Book Engine Dependency Boundary](../designs/open/player-experience-refoundation/phase-2/09-BOOK-ENGINE-DEPENDENCY-BOUNDARY.md) as an **open design study**, strongly favoring an in-house book shell, semantic reader and source-to-leaf mapping; treat page-turn mechanics as an optional replaceable effect. Physical page numbers must never become canonical passage IDs or durable bookmarks. Keep continuous reading as fallback and design index/history independently. A disposable A/B/C study (minimal in-house CSS turn vs OpenPageFlip vs Gul Labs) with long prose/interactive choices, 200% zoom, RTL, update mid-turn, 2,000 passages and explicit package-removal drill is needed before any dependency decision. No third-party package adopted and no implementation authorized.

### Reader-book clarification: keep the book the same across fictional worlds

After inspecting the adaptive-leaf prototype and discussing page-turn packages, the owner refined the artistic goal: a reader **always reads a recognizable book**, with the fictional scene staged around it. A dark cave may cast blue light on the book; a starship battle may put stars and explosive light in the background; a microscopic consciousness may have an abstract environment. None requires the **book itself** to morph into a cell membrane, cockpit terminal or torn field diary. Sparse scene-controlled text emphasis and source-appropriate illustrations remain eligible; legibility and book/navigation identity remain stable. A book has two facing leaves when spread, but neither two text columns nor “right page always illustration” should be required; long prose flows across derived leaves, art is optional and choices remain attached to current narrative. Mobile should offer one leaf. The owner also again rejected permanent left-hand tabs and developer-facing inspector chrome.
